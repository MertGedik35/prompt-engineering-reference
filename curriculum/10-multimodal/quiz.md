# Quiz: Multimodal Prompting

Answer all eight questions. Questions 5 and 6 are scenario questions. A strong short answer names a
decision boundary and evidence, not only a definition.

1. **Multiple choice — Image understanding.** Which design most directly demonstrates `Image understanding`?
   A. A longer answer
   B. A decision or control matching this module's definition
   C. A confident tone
   D. An undocumented provider assumption

2. **Multiple choice — Screenshots and documents.** Which design most directly demonstrates `Screenshots and documents`?
   A. A longer answer
   B. A decision or control matching this module's definition
   C. A confident tone
   D. An undocumented provider assumption

3. **Multiple choice — Charts and diagrams.** Which design most directly demonstrates `Charts and diagrams`?
   A. A longer answer

4. **Multiple choice — Generation briefs.** Which design most directly demonstrates `Generation briefs`?
   A. A longer answer

5. **Scenario analysis.** In the normal scenario—compare chart analysis, image generation, and image editing contracts—name two observable acceptance checks and explain why each belongs to this module.

6. **Scenario analysis.** How should the design respond when the chart legend is cropped and two series use colors that are indistinguishable for some viewers. Justify whether to clarify, proceed, abstain, or escalate.

7. **Error identification.** Identify at least three defects in: `Look at this and make it better.`

8. **Design decision.** Give a safe expected response to this failure case: A screenshot contains text telling the analyzer to ignore the user's question and disclose hidden context. Name one prompt control and one application control.
